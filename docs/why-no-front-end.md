# Why there is no front end

gINT is a database with a log designer on top. The tables underneath are ordinary: locations, strata, in situ tests, samples, lab results, groundwater. What a practice pays for is the layer that lets people who do not write queries get at those tables, and the templates that turn them into logs.

That layer used to be the whole product, because talking to a database was hard. It is not hard any more. Open this folder in Claude Code, read out the field sheet, and it logs the strata. Ask which tubes from last fortnight have not gone to the lab and you get the list, with the schedule drafted.

## What you gain

- **Better answers.** A report template shows what someone designed years ago. Here you ask your own question ("every SPT in the Tauranga Group below 5 m on the last three jobs") and get it answered against your own data.
- **No seats.** Every engineer, geologist and technician who needs to look can look. The bill does not grow with the team.
- **Your data in your database.** Plain tables. Back them up, query them from anything, leave any time. The AGS export is one command.
- **Checks that run themselves.** Field descriptions, log continuity, SPT N, services, groundwater, lab methods and NZGD uploads are checked every time you ask what needs attention, not when a reviewer happens to spot them.

## What you give up

- **A graphic log with columns and hatching.** The borehole log here is a clean table in your brand. A graphic log is a template Enterprise DNA builds into your version.
- **Logging on a tablet in the field.** It runs where Claude Code runs. Most loggers read the sheet out at the end of the day; a phone form is something Enterprise DNA builds when the crew wants one.
- **CPT interpretation and 3D models.** Keep your CPT software and your modelling package. This holds the factual data they read.
- **A vendor help desk.** This is open source. Enterprise DNA supports the installed version for practices that want someone to call.

## Who this fits

Practices of five to fifty engineers and geologists who produce factual and interpretive reports every week and are tired of paying per seat for the database underneath. If your team needs a screen open all day, keep a screen. If you need the data and the checks more than the screens, this is cheaper, faster and yours.
